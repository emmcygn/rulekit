R&D spending has outpaced drug approvals.

Protocol changes add cost and delay to clinical trials. RuleKit explores how teams review eligibility criteria and their amendments.

A historical decline in R&D productivity

A 2012 analysis reported an approximately 80-fold decline in new drug approvals per inflation-adjusted R&D dollar since 1950. The authors called the trend Eroom's law. The bars illustrate the direction, not annual cost estimates.

Scannell et al., 2012
From research spending to trial operations

Clinical trials require site work, participant visits, data collection and review. The protocol specifies how that work should be carried out. A substantial amendment can change documents, systems and site procedures.

The part this prototype addresses

Eligibility criteria become checklists, queries and screening decisions. RuleKit makes the encoded interpretation and its changes visible for review. It has not demonstrated lower trial costs or fewer amendments.

RuleKit is a prototype for clinical operations teams and engineers working on trial eligibility. This walkthrough uses invented documents and synthetic patients.

01the clinical side

Protocol amendments carry implementation costs.

A 2016 study reported median direct costs of $141K in Phase II and $535K in Phase III for a substantial amendment.

These are amendment costs, not savings demonstrated by RuleKit. The study examined 836 protocols from 15 companies; the direct cost analysis used a subset of 52 protocols.

Sponsors judged 45% of substantial amendments avoidable. That describes the share of amendments, not the share of spending, and does not establish that eligibility logic caused them.

Getz et al., Therapeutic Innovation & Regulatory Science, 2016

“At least 30” includes 30. “Over 30” does not.

Compare the source criterion with its checklist or query, then test the boundary.

A criterion may include a threshold, timing requirements and clinical judgement. Each needs to be represented explicitly or recorded as unmodeled.

05the build

Review the implementation and its limits.

The next evaluation needs clinical operations experience: realistic screening steps, ambiguous criteria and amendment review requirements.

Development so far uses public registry text and synthetic patients. The project has not been evaluated in a real screening workflow.

The draft rule format defines behavior that the engine can be checked against. The repository includes regression testskeep fixed bugs fixed for boundary cases and earlier implementation errors. Three examples:

conflict detectionRegression cases in tests/adversarial/conflicts.test.ts check for false positives in the static conflict checker.
consistent countsThe report and workbench share src/core/attrition.ts. Regression tests compare their counts over the same inputs.
missing evidencetests/adversarial/kleene.test.ts checks three-valued logic, including that an absent value stays unknown.

A useful next step is one trial pack reviewed with a clinical operations collaborator, including an explicit record of criteria that remain unmodeled.
